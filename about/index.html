About

Vlad Svoboda

I’m Vlad Svoboda. I’m open to collaborations, commissions and projects that connect with the work you find here. If something makes you think we should make something together, I’d like to hear from you.

This is a collection of work from roughly the past ten years, since I left finance and started putting my time into things I wanted to make. Films, writing, participatory art, theatre, experiments with technology. Some of it worked beautifully. Some of it didn’t do what I hoped. A lot of it happened with other people, and their part in it matters.

I’m putting it together for myself first. At certain points I’ve had so many things going on at once that I barely had time to understand one before I was deep into the next. Memory is a funny thing. You remember the disaster, or the party afterwards, and forget a whole project. Bringing the work into one place is a way to look at what I’ve actually been doing, what I keep coming back to, and what I want to do next.

Before all this, there was IT, organising parties, and about a decade in finance. I brought some useful things out of that life: getting people together, finding resources, keeping something moving when it looks like it’s about to fall apart. I also brought an impressive capacity to underestimate how difficult something was going to be. The Ocean Within, a film that has occupied much of this period, gave me a very thorough education in that.

The interests look scattered until you spend a bit of time with them. I keep coming back to how people work: what makes us feel connected, what we’re willing to do for a reward, what stops us from speaking, and how much our surroundings shape our behaviour. Sometimes that becomes a film. Sometimes it’s an experience you walk into and become part of. Sometimes it’s a piece of writing that takes me years to understand.

Acting led me to help establish a school for non-professionals in Kyiv, because I found it such a powerful way to explore being human. An interest in how we live led to a collaboration on a contemporary Ukrainian kurin outside Kyiv. Writing has been there through all of it, and I’m working on a novel. There are still parts of this story to add.

I call this a laboratory because trying things is the part that holds it together. I want to show the work, but also leave room for what happened around it: the decisions, the mistakes, the unexpected results, and the ways I’ve changed my mind. Making that public feels more useful than trying to squeeze it all into a neat professional identity. And if it leads to the next interesting conversation, even better.
