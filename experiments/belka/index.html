EEG / virtual reality / 2016

Belka

Could someone else step into a world shaped by your brain’s signals?

Two people. One changing world.

At Hack the Senses in London, I pitched an EEG-driven virtual environment. A team formed around the idea, using an HTC Vive and my computer to build a working prototype over the weekend.

One person wore the EEG sensor. Another wore the headset. The virtual world changed colour in response to the signal. I assembled a stimulus video with contrasting images and music; we also used the smell of freshly brewed coffee.

What we made

The ambitious plan for complex environments became a simpler, open world whose colours changed. That part worked. An attempt to generate a responsive musical soundtrack did not come together.

The team received the Intel Code for Good prize as a runner-up. The project later became an unexpected social experiment at a technology conference: people trying to demonstrate how calm they were, in front of an increasingly interested audience.

Contributions

Original concept, team lead, stimulus design and video editing. Built with Team Belka, including Doniyor Ulmasov.

Looking back Intentions, outcomes, and what I learned.

The simplest part of the prototype became its most interesting feature. Once visitors understood the colour mapping, they began responding to it—and to one another. That is the part I remember most clearly. It was an experiment in communication, rather than a reliable instrument for reading emotions.
