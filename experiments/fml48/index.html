Film competition / development / 2018–2019

FML48

Give a group of filmmakers a brief, forty-eight hours and a reason to get together. See what comes back.

A film in a weekend

FML48 was a filmmaking competition we organised through Moscow Misfits in July 2018. The task was to make a complete short film, from the first idea to the finished edit, in forty-eight hours. Five minutes maximum, including the credits. Enough time to make something, and very little time to keep putting it off.

The brief asked people to imagine arriving on Earth for the first time. The camera was the eyes of a visitor who looked human and knew nothing about the place. Its only motive was curiosity. That was the starting point; the filmmakers had to decide what this person would notice and what kind of story would follow.

The production weekend ran from 20 to 22 July. Thirty films from eleven cities made the shortlist. We advertised a million roubles in development support for the winning team's next project, together with professional help. We also put together talks and a programme around the awards. Making the short was a way into a larger conversation about how people get films made.

What people made

The Best Film award went to Первый день / The First Day, directed by Alexandra Sokolovskaya, which also received Best Actress. Five other films shared the remaining awards. These are the teams' films, collected on the competition's original channel:

  • Первый день / The First Day — Best Film and Best Actress; directed by Alexandra Sokolovskaya.
  • Наш — Best Comedy and Best Actor; directed by Vera Smolina.
  • Сын — Best Original Soundtrack; directed by Andrey Vishnyakov.
  • Ловец любви — Best Cinematography; directed by Marina Ganyar.
  • Ключевой элемент — Best Screenplay; screenplay by Alexey Kostyrin and Anton Baydyonok.
  • Любопытство — Best Arthouse; directed by Tayuta Nikitina.

After the weekend

The work continued into 2019. Ani and I read and discussed scripts with the winning team and considered what could be developed into an international coproduction. Alexandra wrote about that process herself, including the delays and the difference between winning development support and getting a film into production. Her account of winning and what came next gives the view from the other side of those conversations.

Alongside this, we published writing and organised conversations under RTFM. In February 2019, I wrote an explanation of development: rights, scripts, budgets, financing and all the work between an idea and permission to start shooting. The original Russian article, #ФМЛ48 × РТФМ vol. 2, survives, as do the competition films and recorded talks.

Looking back Development, and learning it the hard way.

Writing about development while still in it

By the time I wrote that article, I had spent years trying to make Aquatilis, the film that eventually became The Ocean Within. We had already shot a great deal, struggled to find the story and made a cut I didn't think worked. I was explaining a process I had learned by getting quite a lot of it backwards.

The article ends with the possibility that development goes nowhere. You can do the work, spend the money and still never get the film made. I also wrote that you would at least get invaluable experience, whether you wanted it or not. That part is fairly recognisable in my own story.
