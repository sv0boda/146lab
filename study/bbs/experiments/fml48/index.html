Film competition / development / 2018–2019

FML48

Give a group of filmmakers a brief, forty-eight hours and a reason to get together. See what comes back.

↓ Looking back — what I think about it now

A film in a weekend

FML48 was a filmmaking competition we organised through Moscow Misfits in July 2018. The task was to make a complete short film, from the first idea to the finished edit, in forty-eight hours. Five minutes maximum, including the credits. Enough time to make something, and very little time to keep putting it off.

The brief asked people to imagine arriving on Earth for the first time. The camera was the eyes of a visitor who looked human and knew nothing about the place. Its only motive was curiosity. That was the starting point; the filmmakers had to decide what this person would notice and what kind of story would follow.

The production weekend ran from 20 to 22 July. We received over sixty submissions; thirty films from eleven cities made the shortlist. We spent almost nothing on promotion. I think our entire experiment with paid ads came to about seven dollars. We paid for the website, found sponsors and somehow ended up with a proper event.

A room full of filmmakers

Ani and I designed the awards: Chinese lucky cats, each turned into a trophy. We put together talks and an award ceremony, and made it a community event. People had spent a weekend making something under quite absurd time pressure. Now they could meet, watch the work and celebrate it together.

I remember the reception being wonderful. I'm very proud of what we made happen, especially with so little money. Moscow Misfits didn't become the business we had hoped for, but FML48 worked.

What people made

The Best Film award went to Первый день / The First Day, directed by Alexandra Sokolovskaya, which also received Best Actress. Five other films shared the remaining awards. These are the teams' films, collected on the competition's original channel:

  • Первый день / The First Day — Best Film and Best Actress; directed by Alexandra Sokolovskaya.
  • Наш — Best Comedy and Best Actor; directed by Vera Smolina.
  • Сын — Best Original Soundtrack; directed by Andrey Vishnyakov.
  • Ловец любви — Best Cinematography; directed by Marina Ganyar.
  • Ключевой элемент — Best Screenplay; screenplay by Alexey Kostyrin and Anton Baydyonok.
  • Любопытство — Best Arthouse; directed by Tayuta Nikitina.

The film I thought would win

I thought Наш / Nash, directed by Vera Smolina, was going to take Best Film. The jury chose The First Day instead. Nash won Best Comedy and Best Actor, and I still want to give it a place here.

Vera's work afterwards

Vera went on to co-direct the comedy series Настя, соберись!, released in 2021, with Konstantin Mayer. Her short Парад планет / Parade of Planets was also included in the 2021 anthology Я передумала / I Changed My Mind. This interview with Vera and the other anthology directors gives them room to talk about making their work. It is lovely to be able to follow someone's work beyond the weekend when you first encountered it.

After the weekend

The million-rouble prize was for developing a project. Ani and I read and discussed the winning team's scripts, chose a feature to work on, and spent a great deal of time trying to get it somewhere.

The project was Завещание обжоры / The Glutton's Will, based on a play by Mikhail Sokolovsky, Alexandra Sokolovskaya's writing partner. We worked towards an international coproduction, presented it and pitched it. That route didn't come together. In the end, our work helped get the screenplay optioned by a Russian producer. I remember it being optioned twice, although I would need to check the agreements to be certain. It was never greenlit or produced.

That was disappointing, but the work did lead to further conversations for the filmmakers. An option was a real step, even if it wasn't the film we had hoped to help make. Alexandra's account of winning and beginning the development process tells the earlier part from her side.

Alongside this, we published writing and organised conversations under RTFM. In February 2019, I wrote #ФМЛ48 × РТФМ vol. 2, an explanation of development: rights, scripts, budgets, financing and all the work between an idea and permission to start shooting.

Writing about development while still in it

By the time I wrote that article, I had spent years trying to make Aquatilis, the film that eventually became The Ocean Within. We had already shot a great deal, struggled to find the story and made a cut I didn't think worked. I was explaining a process I had learned by getting quite a lot of it backwards.

The article ends with the possibility that development goes nowhere. You can do the work, spend the money and still never get the film made. I also wrote that you would at least get invaluable experience, whether you wanted it or not. That part is fairly recognisable in my own story.
