Experimental film / collaboration / 2020

BMW 850 Ci

I thought there might be a car show I wanted to make. This was our attempt to find it.

↓ Looking back — what I think about it now

Kyiv, 2020

I commissioned this pilot with my friend Eugene Andrushenko in Kyiv. We were trying to make automotive content with a deliberately strange flavour. I wasn’t especially interested in making a traditional car show, but I wanted to try something with cars, and there was plenty of room for caricature. We made quite a lot of room for it.

We split the writing. Eugene wrote the structure and narrative and directed the film. I contributed the automotive material and appeared in it. The BMW 850 Ci was the subject of our pilot, which never became a series.

Update · November 2026

In early 2026, Eugene — Yujin to me — was picked up by mobilisation officers and is now serving in the Armed Forces of Ukraine.

The Russian invasion froze his world long before that. For the first couple of years, he struggled to stay afloat. The kind of experimental work he makes needs peacetime, and suddenly there was very little room for it. Eventually he found a way to support himself through commercial work. Then he was mobilised.

I have friends, like Mykola from Kurin, who chose to enlist and seem to have found their place in that work. Yujin’s experience has been different. He is in a safe posting and has no complaints about where he serves, but his artistic life is on hold. Looking at this ridiculous car film now, I also see a memory of an easier time. A time when we could spend our energy trying something strange with a BMW, and the question was whether it was any good.

Looking backWhat worked, what didn’t, and what changed.

On deliberate caricature, unintended awkwardness, and my contribution to the boring bits.

The car-nerd bits were mine

Looking at it now, I can see that Eugene’s narrative is what keeps the thing moving. It gives you something to follow, even when the whole thing gets very awkward. Then my car-nerd material comes along and makes me want to fall asleep. On the page, it looked okay. In the film, I can see what I wanted to say, but I don’t particularly want to sit through it. That is a fairly useful thing to discover about your own writing.

I still find the result funny in an awkward way. It almost gets to the point of being so bad that it becomes good, although I can’t claim that was the plan. The caricature was deliberate. This particular flavour of awkwardness wasn’t quite what I had in mind, and even I don’t feel we found what we were after.

I’m not ashamed of it. I also don’t feel any need to pretend it worked better than it did. It remains an experiment, and the part that interests me now is the writing: how something can seem perfectly reasonable on a page and then lose the person who has to watch it. I still have very little interest in making a conventional car show, but that question follows me into other things.
